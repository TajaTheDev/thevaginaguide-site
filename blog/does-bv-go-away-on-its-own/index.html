Does BV go away on its own? When you can wait, and when you shouldn't

By Taylor, women's health educator · November 27, 2026 · 3 min read

Quick answer

Sometimes. Mild BV-like changes, especially around your period, can clear without treatment. But many cases don't clear for weeks, and untreated BV raises your risk of STIs and pregnancy complications. The CDC recommends treating anyone with symptoms, usually with metronidazole or clindamycin.

You noticed the smell. Maybe some thin gray discharge too. And your first thought was probably: can I just wait this out? Fair question. Nobody loves booking an appointment for something that might disappear by Friday. The honest answer is: sometimes BV does clear on its own, but often it doesn't, and there are good reasons not to just hope.

Yes, BV can clear up by itself (sometimes)

BV isn't a germ you catch. It's a shift in the bacteria that normally live in your vagina: the protective Lactobacillus drops and other bacteria take over. Those shifts come and go more than most people realize. In one study that tracked women with frequent swabs, most BV-like shifts that started during a period were short-lived.

In a large trial of pregnant women with BV who got a placebo instead of medicine, about 13% cleared within 2 to 4 weeks, and about 36% after 10 weeks or more. So yes, it happens. But flip those numbers around: most women in that study were still dealing with BV weeks later.

Why waiting it out is a gamble

BV feels like a nuisance, but it isn't harmless. According to the CDC, women with BV are at higher risk of:

  • Picking up STIs, including HIV, chlamydia, gonorrhea, trichomoniasis and herpes.
  • Pregnancy complications, such as preterm birth.
  • Infections after gynecologic procedures, like an IUD insertion or surgery.

That's why the CDC recommends treatment for everyone with symptoms, pregnant or not.

When it's reasonable to wait a few days

  • The change showed up right around your period and is mild.
  • You have no symptoms at all and it was found by accident. Treatment usually isn't needed for BV without symptoms if you're not pregnant.
  • You've had BV before, recognize it, and it's mild and fading.

Give it a few days, not a few weeks. If the odor or discharge is still there after your period ends, it's not clearing on its own.

When not to wait

  • You're pregnant or trying to get pregnant.
  • You have itching, burning, sores, pelvic pain or fever. That could be something else, like yeast, trich or an STI.
  • You have a procedure coming up (IUD, biopsy, surgery).
  • Symptoms have lasted more than a week, or this is your 3rd time or more this year.
  • You've never been diagnosed before. Get tested once so you know what you're dealing with. (Not sure? Read Yeast infection or BV? How to tell the difference.)

What actually treats BV

The proven treatments are prescription antibiotics. The CDC-recommended options are metronidazole pills for 7 days, metronidazole gel for 5 days, or clindamycin cream for 7 days. Short-term cure rates are good. The hard part is that BV comes back for more than half of women within 12 months, which is why treating it once isn't always the end of the story (more on that in Why does BV keep coming back?).

What doesn't help (and can make it worse)

  • Douching or "cleansing" washes. These wash away the good bacteria and can make BV more likely.
  • Garlic, tea tree oil or yogurt inside the vagina. Not proven, and they can irritate.
  • Scented soaps and wipes. See our list of ingredients to keep away from your vagina.
  • Boric acid on its own. It isn't a proven stand-alone BV cure. It has a role in some recurrent-BV plans (here's when).

The bottom line

BV can go away on its own, but it's a coin you don't need to flip. If symptoms last past your period, keep coming back, or you're pregnant, get it treated. It's quick, it's common, and nobody at the clinic is judging you.

Questions people ask

How long can BV last without treatment?

There's no set timeline. Some shifts in vaginal bacteria clear within days, especially around your period. Others hang on for weeks or months. If you have symptoms for more than a few days, get checked instead of waiting it out.

Can BV go away during my period?

Your period raises vaginal pH, so a BV-like shift around your period is common, and many of those shifts are short-lived. If odor or discharge is still there after your period ends, it's probably not going away on its own.

Is untreated BV dangerous?

BV isn't an emergency, but leaving it untreated raises your risk of picking up STIs, including HIV and chlamydia, and it's linked to pregnancy complications and infections after gynecologic procedures. That's why symptomatic BV is treated.

Can I treat BV at home?

Prescription antibiotics (metronidazole or clindamycin) are the proven treatment. Home remedies like garlic, yogurt, tea tree oil or douching aren't proven, and douching can make BV more likely. Some pharmacies and telehealth services can prescribe quickly.

Should I see a doctor if BV keeps coming back?

Yes. BV comes back for more than half of women within a year. If you've had it 3 or more times, ask your provider about a longer plan, such as suppressive treatment, rather than treating each flare from scratch.
