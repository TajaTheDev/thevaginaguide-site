Yeast infection or BV? How to tell the difference (without guessing wrong)

By Taja, women's health educator · November 12, 2026 · 5 min read

Quick answer

A yeast infection usually means itching or burning with thick, white, cottage-cheese discharge and little smell. BV usually means thin gray or white discharge with a fishy smell that's often stronger after sex or your period, with less itching. Symptoms overlap a lot: in one study, only about a third of women buying yeast treatment actually had just a yeast infection.

You're standing in the pharmacy aisle, Googling under your breath, wondering if that box of Monistat is about to save your week or waste $20. Fair. Yeast and BV are the two most common reasons for vaginal symptoms, and they can feel a lot alike.

Why guessing wrong is so common

In a US study of women who were buying over-the-counter yeast treatment, researchers examined them before they used it. Only 33.7% actually had a yeast infection alone. Others had BV, a mix of infections, trichomoniasis, or nothing at all. Even women who'd been diagnosed with yeast before weren't better at spotting it.

Why it matters: yeast medicine does nothing for BV. If you treat the wrong thing, the real problem keeps going while you're stuck itching and confused.

The pH strip trick

Pharmacies sell vaginal pH test strips. A healthy vaginal pH is about 3.8 to 4.5. If yours reads above 4.5, BV or trich becomes more likely than yeast, which usually leaves pH normal. A normal reading doesn't prove it's yeast, though, so think of the strip as a clue, not a diagnosis. (More in What's a normal vaginal pH?)

Could it be both?

Yes. In the same study, about 1 in 5 women had yeast plus another infection. If symptoms improve but don't fully clear, that's a sign to get tested.

When to skip the pharmacy and see a provider

  • It's your first time with these symptoms
  • You're pregnant
  • You have fever, pelvic pain, sores or blisters
  • Over-the-counter treatment didn't work
  • You get yeast infections 3 or more times a year (that's recurrent and needs a different plan)
  • You might have been exposed to an STI

Questions people ask

Does a yeast infection smell fishy?

Usually not. Yeast infections typically have little odor. A fishy smell points more toward BV or trichomoniasis.

Can I use Monistat if it's BV?

No. Monistat (miconazole) is an antifungal and doesn't treat BV, which needs prescription antibiotics. Treating the wrong infection delays relief.

Can you have BV and a yeast infection at the same time?

Yes. In one study, about 1 in 5 women who thought they had yeast actually had yeast plus another infection.

What does BV discharge look like?

BV discharge is usually thin and watery, gray or off-white, with a fishy smell that's often stronger after sex or during your period.
