How to wash your vagina (actually, your vulva) the right way

By Taja, women's health educator · November 12, 2026 · 5 min read

Quick answer

Don't wash inside your vagina. It cleans itself with discharge and good bacteria. Wash only the outside (the vulva) with warm water, or a gentle fragrance-free cleanser if you like, then pat dry. Skip douches, scented washes, wipes and sprays, which can irritate your skin and disrupt the bacteria that keep odor and infections away.

Somewhere along the way, a lot of us learned that "clean" down there means scrubbing, foaming and smelling like a candle. Let's unlearn that, because the cleanest-feeling vagina is usually the one you leave alone.

Vagina vs vulva: quick anatomy check

Your vagina is the internal canal. Your vulva is everything on the outside: the labia, clitoris and the opening of the vagina. When people say "wash your vagina," they should mean your vulva. The inside handles itself.

Your vagina is self-cleaning

Discharge carries out old cells and keeps your vagina clean, while good bacteria called lactobacilli keep things acidic so unwanted bacteria can't take over. When you wash inside, you wash away the helpers. That's why the U.S. Office on Women's Health advises using only warm water to clean the outside and notes that even mild soap can irritate.

How to wash your vulva, step by step

  1. Warm water, your hand. In the shower, rinse the vulva and the folds between your labia with warm water.
  2. Cleanser is optional. If you want one, use a gentle, fragrance-free cleanser on the outer skin only, and rinse well. No soap inside the vagina, ever.
  3. Front to back. Wash and wipe from front to back so bacteria from your bottom stay away from your vagina and urethra.
  4. Pat dry. Gently, with a clean towel. Moisture trapped in skin folds can lead to irritation.
  5. Let it breathe. Cotton underwear, and consider sleeping without underwear.

Why douching backfires

Douching flushes water or other fluids inside the vagina. It feels "fresh" for an hour and causes problems for weeks. It's linked with vaginal irritation, BV, STIs and pelvic inflammatory disease, and women who douche weekly are five times more likely to develop BV. It doesn't prevent STIs or pregnancy either.

Special situations

  • After sex: pee and rinse the outside with water. That's it.
  • On your period: change pads and tampons regularly and rinse the vulva with water. A little metallic smell is normal.
  • After a workout: get out of sweaty leggings fast and rinse or change.
  • Odor that won't go away: washing more won't fix it. A fishy smell usually means BV or trich, which need treatment. Decode the smell in why does my vagina smell?

What about "pH-balanced" washes?

If a fragrance-free, gentle external wash makes you feel better, it's fine on the outside. But no wash fixes your internal pH. Your lactobacilli do that job, and the more you leave them alone, the better they do it. Check any product against our list of 15 ingredients to avoid.

Questions people ask

Should you wash inside your vagina?

No. The vagina cleans itself with discharge and good bacteria. Washing inside, including douching, can disrupt that balance and is linked with BV and irritation.

Do I need soap to wash my vulva?

No. Warm water is enough. If you prefer a cleanser, choose a gentle, fragrance-free one and use it on the outside only.

How often should I wash my vulva?

Once a day with warm water is plenty for most people, plus a rinse after workouts or sex if you like.

Why do I still smell after washing?

A mild smell is normal. A strong fishy or foul smell that doesn't go away after washing usually means an infection like BV or trichomoniasis, which needs treatment.
