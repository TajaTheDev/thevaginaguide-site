Should your partner be treated for BV? The 2025 study that changed the conversation

By Taja, women's health educator · November 12, 2026 · 5 min read

Quick answer

If you have a regular male partner, it's worth asking. In a 2025 trial published in the New England Journal of Medicine, BV came back within 12 weeks in 35% of women whose partners were treated too, compared with 63% when only the woman was treated. Partners took metronidazole pills plus clindamycin cream on the penis for 7 days.

For decades the advice for BV was simple: treat the woman, leave the partner alone. And for decades, women kept getting BV back. A 2025 study finally tested what a lot of us suspected all along: maybe he's part of the problem.

Wait, can men carry BV bacteria?

Men don't get "BV" the way women do, but they can carry BV-associated bacteria on the penile skin and inside the urethra. During sex, those bacteria can be passed back to you, even after your treatment worked. That's the ping-pong effect.

What the study did

The trial, nicknamed StepUp, enrolled 164 monogamous couples in Australia where the woman had BV. All the women got standard first-line treatment. In half the couples, the male partner was also treated for 7 days with:

  • Metronidazole 400 mg tablets, twice a day
  • 2% clindamycin cream applied to the penile skin, twice a day

Couples were asked to treat at the same time and avoid sex until both had finished.

What it found

35%BV came back when the partner was treated
63%BV came back when only the woman was treated

The difference was so clear that the trial's safety board stopped the study early: treating only the woman was inferior. The researchers at Monash University described the result as BV recurrence being roughly halved.

Does this mean BV is an STI?

The study's authors argue that BV behaves like a sexually transmitted infection, and the results support that. Official guidelines haven't fully caught up with labeling it one. The practical takeaway matters more than the label: if you have a regular partner, your treatment plan should include them.

What about female partners?

BV can pass between women too. Earlier research found having a female partner was linked with BV coming back. The 2025 trial only studied male partners, so there isn't a tested regimen for female partners yet. Ask your provider about testing and treating both of you.

How to bring it up with your doctor

Copy this into your notes app:

"I've had BV more than once. I read about the 2025 NEJM StepUp trial where treating male partners with oral metronidazole and clindamycin cream cut recurrence from 63% to 35%. Can my partner be treated at the same time as me?"

Your partner can also ask his own doctor and bring the study. If a provider says no, ask why, and ask what else you can do to prevent recurrence.

The fine print

  • The study included couples in monogamous relationships. Results may differ with new or multiple partners.
  • Both medications can have side effects. Partners should check with a provider before taking anything.
  • Condoms during and after treatment still help protect your reset.

Questions people ask

Can my boyfriend give me BV?

He can carry BV-associated bacteria and pass them back to you during sex. That's why treating male partners at the same time cut BV recurrence from 63% to 35% in a 2025 trial.

What medicine do partners take for BV?

In the 2025 trial, male partners took metronidazole 400 mg tablets twice daily plus 2% clindamycin cream applied to the penile skin twice daily, both for 7 days. A provider has to prescribe it.

Should we avoid sex during BV treatment?

In the study, couples were asked to avoid sex until both had finished treatment. Using condoms for a few weeks afterward also helps your vaginal pH reset.

Is BV an STI now?

Researchers argue BV behaves like a sexually transmitted infection, and partner treatment results support that. Official guidelines haven't fully reclassified it yet.
