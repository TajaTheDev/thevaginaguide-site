Do Kegels improve sex? What the research actually says

By Taja Moxey, women's health educator · November 20, 2026 · 6 min read

Quick answer

Pelvic floor exercises can help. A 2024 review of randomized trials found pelvic floor muscle training improved women's sexual function scores, including arousal, orgasm and satisfaction, though the studies were small and the evidence is still early. These muscles contract during orgasm and add to grip and sensation. The goal is strong and responsive, not tight: train the squeeze and the release, and skip vaginal tightening creams and sticks.

"Can Kegels actually make sex better?" is one of the most common questions in my DMs. Short answer: they can help, and here's what the research says, plus how to train for it the right way.

How your pelvic floor affects sex

Your pelvic floor is a group of muscles that wraps around your vagina, urethra and anus. During sex it does three things:

  • Grip. Squeezing these muscles increases friction and sensation, for you and a partner.
  • Blood flow. Active, well-trained muscles support the blood flow that drives arousal and natural lubrication.
  • Orgasm. The pelvic floor contracts in rhythm during orgasm. Stronger, more responsive muscles may mean stronger sensations.

What the research says

21randomized trials reviewed (2024)
↑arousal, orgasm + satisfaction

A 2024 systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized trials found that pelvic floor muscle training improved women's overall sexual function scores, including arousal, orgasm and satisfaction. The researchers rated the certainty of evidence as low because the studies were small and varied, but no side effects were reported.

A 2025 review focused on women after menopause found similar benefits for orgasm, arousal and satisfaction. Earlier reviews found most studies showed improvement in at least one area of sexual function, especially in women who also had leaking or other pelvic floor symptoms.

Bottom line: it's not a miracle fix, but it's safe, free, and the research points in the right direction.

How to train for better sex

  1. Find the right muscles. Imagine stopping gas and pee at the same time, then lift up and in. Your butt and thighs stay relaxed. Our step-by-step Kegel guide walks you through it.
  2. Train strength. Slow holds of 3 seconds, building to 10, with a full release after each one.
  3. Train control. Quick flicks (1 second on, 1 second off) and the "elevator" (squeeze in 3 steps up, release in 3 steps down).
  4. Train the release. On an inhale, let your pelvic floor soften and drop. Being able to relax on purpose makes sex more comfortable.
  5. Use it during sex. Try a few gentle squeezes during penetration, then relax completely. Keep breathing.

Most women notice a stronger squeeze after 6 to 8 weeks of regular training.

Strong, not "tight"

A healthy vagina isn't tight. It's strong and responsive: it can grip on purpose and relax on purpose. If sex hurts, your pelvic floor may already be too tight, and more squeezing can make it worse. Focus on release, and see a pelvic floor physical therapist.

And please skip "vaginal tightening" creams, sticks and herbs. Many work by drying or irritating the tissue, which can make sex more painful and raise your risk of infection.

See a provider if

  • Sex is painful, or you can't insert a tampon comfortably
  • You bleed after sex
  • You notice new discharge, odor, sores or itching
  • Changes in desire or sensation started with a new medication

Questions people ask

Do Kegels make you tighter?

They build strength and control, and many women notice more grip and sensation. The goal is a pelvic floor that's strong and responsive, not tight. If sex is painful, your pelvic floor may already be too tight.

Can pelvic floor exercises help with orgasm?

A 2024 review of randomized trials found pelvic floor muscle training improved arousal, orgasm and satisfaction scores, though the evidence is still early. These muscles contract in rhythm during orgasm.

How long do Kegels take to work for sex?

Most women notice a stronger squeeze after about 6 to 8 weeks of regular, correct training.

Are vaginal tightening products safe?

Most aren't a good idea. Many tightening creams, sticks and herbs work by drying or irritating the tissue, which can cause pain and raise infection risk.
