Why antibiotics give you a yeast infection (and what to do next time)

By Taja Moxey, women's health educator · November 12, 2026 · 5 min read

Quick answer

Antibiotics can wipe out the Lactobacillus that normally keep Candida yeast in check, so yeast overgrows. In one trial, about 23% of women got a yeast infection after a short course of antibiotics. Surprisingly, probiotics didn't prevent it in that trial. If it happens every time, ask your prescriber about a plan before you start your next course.

Antibiotics fix one problem and, for a lot of us, start another. Strep throat? UTI? Sinus infection? You take the pills and three days later the itching starts. It's not in your head, and it's not bad luck.

Why antibiotics trigger yeast infections

Your vagina is home to Lactobacillus, the good bacteria that keep things acidic and keep yeast in its lane. Most antibiotics can't tell good bacteria from bad. When they clear out Lactobacillus, Candida (the yeast that's often already living there quietly) gets room to grow.

How often does it happen? In an Australian trial of women taking short antibiotic courses, 23% developed a yeast infection afterward.

The probiotic plot twist

Here's the part that surprises people. That same trial tested whether taking Lactobacillus probiotics by mouth, vaginally, or both could prevent post-antibiotic yeast infections. They didn't. The trial was stopped early because there was no effect.

That doesn't mean probiotics are useless for everything, and new strains are being studied. It means you shouldn't count on yogurt or a probiotic capsule as your only shield.

What actually helps

  1. Only take antibiotics you truly need. Ask your provider if an antibiotic is necessary, especially for things that are often viral.
  2. Tell your prescriber you get yeast infections after antibiotics. If it happens every time, ask whether a preventive antifungal makes sense for you.
  3. Keep things dry and breathable. Cotton underwear, change out of sweaty leggings fast, and skip scented products down there.
  4. Treat early if you know the signs. If you've been diagnosed with yeast before and this feels exactly the same, over-the-counter antifungals work for most uncomplicated infections.
  5. Track it. Note when it happens. Getting 3 or more yeast infections in a year counts as recurrent and deserves a doctor's visit and a longer treatment plan.

Make sure it's really yeast

Antibiotics can also shift things toward other infections, and symptoms overlap. In one study, only about a third of women who bought yeast treatment actually had a yeast infection alone. If it smells fishy, doesn't respond to treatment, or keeps coming back, get checked. Our yeast vs BV guide breaks down the differences.

Questions people ask

How long after antibiotics does a yeast infection start?

It often shows up during the antibiotic course or within a few days after finishing, once Lactobacillus levels have dropped.

Do probiotics prevent yeast infections from antibiotics?

In a randomized trial, oral and vaginal Lactobacillus probiotics did not prevent post-antibiotic yeast infections, and the trial stopped early for lack of effect.

Can I take fluconazole with antibiotics to prevent a yeast infection?

Some providers prescribe a preventive antifungal for people who get yeast infections every time they take antibiotics. Ask your prescriber whether that makes sense for you.

Which antibiotics cause yeast infections?

Broad-spectrum antibiotics are the usual culprits because they affect a wide range of bacteria, including the protective Lactobacillus in the vagina.
