C-section recovery week by week: what's normal and what helps

By Taja Moxey, women's health educator · November 20, 2026 · 7 min read

Quick answer

C-section recovery usually takes about 6 weeks for the incision and daily life, and several months for the deeper tissue and full strength. Walk early and often, lift nothing heavier than your baby at first, and hold a pillow over your incision when you cough. Gentle breathing and pelvic floor exercises are usually fine early; core work and scar massage start once your provider clears you, often around 6 weeks. Numbness and a pouch above the scar are common. Call your provider for spreading redness, pus, an opening wound, fever or worsening pain.

You had major abdominal surgery and went home with a newborn the same week. No wonder you have questions. When can you lift the car seat? Why is your scar numb? What's that pouch above it? Here's what recovery usually looks like, week by week. Your surgeon's advice always comes first, because every recovery is different.

Days 1 to 3: in the hospital

  • Get moving early. Walking as soon as your team says it's OK helps with gas pain, blood flow and healing.
  • Splint your incision. Hold a pillow firmly over your belly when you cough, sneeze, laugh or stand up.
  • Stay ahead of pain. Taking pain medicine on schedule makes it easier to move, feed your baby and rest.

Weeks 1 to 2: home and healing

  • Short, gentle walks around the house, adding a little each day.
  • Lift nothing heavier than your baby. Ask for help with laundry, groceries and older kids.
  • Roll onto your side to get out of bed, then push up with your arms. Skip the "sit-up" move.
  • Try connection breaths: breathe in and let your belly and pelvic floor relax, breathe out and gently lift.
  • Ask your provider when it's safe to drive. You need to be able to brake hard and turn without pain.

Weeks 2 to 6: building up

  • Longer walks, as you feel able.
  • Gentle Kegels and pelvic floor relaxation. Your pelvic floor carried your baby for 9 months too.
  • Still no crunches, heavy lifting, or anything that makes your belly bulge or strain.
  • Numbness around the scar is very common. Small skin nerves are cut during surgery, and feeling often returns slowly over months.

Week 6: your check-up

Bring a list. Ask: "Am I cleared for core work? For scar massage? For exercise? For sex?" And ask for a referral to a pelvic floor physical therapist. In many countries that's routine after every birth. In the US, you usually have to ask.

Weeks 6 to 12: rebuilding

  • Deep core work: heel slides, one-leg dead bugs, bridges and bird dogs, with no doming of your belly. Check for an ab gap with our diastasis recti guide.
  • Scar massage, once the incision is fully closed and you're cleared. Start with gentle circles on the skin above and below the scar, then move onto the scar itself, 5 to 10 minutes a day.
  • Low-impact cardio like walking, a stationary bike or swimming, once your wound is fully healed.

Week 12 and beyond: getting strong

Squats, lifting and carrying your baby with good breathing. If you want to run, postnatal running guidelines suggest waiting at least 12 weeks and passing a few simple tests first, like hopping and jogging on the spot with no leaking, heaviness or pain. Many women feel mostly back to normal by 3 to 6 months. It can take longer, and that's OK.

What's that "shelf" above my scar?

A pouch or overhang above a C-section scar is very common. Swelling, loose skin, and scar tissue that pulls the skin inward all play a part. Time, scar massage and deep core work often help. It's not a sign you did anything wrong.

Call your provider right away if

  • Redness around the incision is spreading, or it's hot, swollen or oozing pus
  • The incision starts to open
  • You have a fever, chills, or pain that's getting worse instead of better
  • You have heavy bleeding, a painful swollen leg, chest pain or trouble breathing
  • You feel very low, anxious or have thoughts of harming yourself or your baby

Questions people ask

How long does it take to recover from a C-section?

The incision and everyday activity usually take about 6 weeks. Deeper tissue keeps healing for months, and many women feel mostly back to normal by 3 to 6 months.

When can I start exercising after a C-section?

Walking starts right away. Gentle breathing and pelvic floor exercises are usually fine early on. Core work and harder exercise start once your provider clears you, often around 6 weeks.

When can I start C-section scar massage?

Once the incision is fully closed with no scabs or oozing, and your provider says it's OK, often around 6 to 8 weeks.

Is numbness around a C-section scar normal?

Yes. Small skin nerves are cut during surgery. Feeling often comes back slowly over months, though some numbness can last.

Why do I have a pouch above my C-section scar?

Swelling, loose skin and scar tissue that tethers the skin all contribute. Time, scar massage and deep core work often help.
