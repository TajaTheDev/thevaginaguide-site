15 ingredients to avoid in feminine hygiene products (and what to use instead)

By Taja, women's health educator · November 12, 2026 · 7 min read

Quick answer

The ingredients most worth skipping near your vulva and vagina are fragrance, essential oils, menthol, harsh foaming detergents (SLS and SLES), antiseptics, DIY douches like vinegar, peroxide, baking soda and garlic, petroleum jelly inside the vagina, nonoxynol-9, very concentrated lubes, benzocaine, the preservative methylisothiazolinone (MI) and talc. For most people, warm water on the outside is all you need.

The feminine care aisle is a wall of pastel bottles promising to make you "fresh." Meanwhile, a lot of the itching, burning and repeat BV in my DMs traces back to... the stuff in those bottles. Your vulva is not your armpit. It's thinner, more absorbent skin sitting next to a delicate ecosystem of good bacteria. Here's what to flip the bottle over and look for.

The 15, grouped by how they mess with you

Things that irritate skin

  • 1. Fragrance / parfum. The top cause of irritation down there. "Unscented" can still hide a masking fragrance, so look for "fragrance-free."
  • 2. Essential oils. Tea tree, peppermint and lavender in "natural" washes and yoni oils can burn and trigger allergic reactions.
  • 3. Menthol and cooling agents. That tingle is irritation on sensitive tissue.
  • 4. SLS / SLES. Harsh foaming detergents in body wash and bubble bath that strip your skin barrier.
  • 5. Benzocaine. Found in anti-itch "feminine" creams. It numbs the itch instead of treating the cause and is a common trigger for allergic rashes.
  • 6. Methylisothiazolinone (MI). A preservative in some wet wipes and washes. It was named Contact Allergen of the Year by the American Contact Dermatitis Society because so many people react to it.

Things that wipe out your good bacteria

  • 7. Antiseptics. Chlorhexidine, povidone-iodine and chloroxylenol (Dettol) washes and baths kill the protective lactobacilli that keep your pH acidic.
  • 8. Hydrogen peroxide, 9. vinegar and 10. baking soda rinses. Any DIY douche is still a douche. The U.S. Office on Women's Health reports that women who douche weekly are five times more likely to develop BV. Baking soda is also alkaline, so it pushes your pH the wrong way.
  • 11. Garlic. A classic DIY yeast "remedy." It can cause chemical burns, and there's no good evidence it works.

Things that don't belong inside you

  • 12. Petroleum jelly (inside). In a U.S. study, women who used petroleum jelly inside the vagina in the past month were 2.2 times more likely to test positive for BV. It also breaks down latex condoms.
  • 13. Nonoxynol-9. The spermicide in some lubes, gels and condoms. It irritates the vaginal lining, and frequent use has been linked with a higher risk of HIV.
  • 14. Very concentrated lubes. Warming, flavored or high-glycerin lubes can pull water out of vaginal tissue and irritate it.

Things to keep away entirely

  • 15. Talc. Found in body and baby powder. In 2024 the WHO's cancer agency classified talc as probably carcinogenic to humans, with limited evidence for ovarian cancer. Skip it down there.

Questions people ask

What ingredients should you avoid in feminine wash?

Skip fragrance or parfum, essential oils, menthol, SLS and SLES, antiseptics like chlorhexidine, and the preservative methylisothiazolinone. A fragrance-free gentle cleanser on the outside, or just warm water, is enough.

Is Vaseline safe to use inside the vagina?

It's not recommended. In a U.S. study, women who used petroleum jelly inside the vagina were 2.2 times more likely to test positive for BV, and it breaks down latex condoms.

Are scented pads bad for you?

Fragrance is the top cause of vulvar irritation, so unscented pads, tampons, period underwear or a cup are gentler choices.

Is it safe to use baby powder down there?

It's best to avoid it. In 2024 the WHO's cancer agency classified talc as probably carcinogenic to humans.
