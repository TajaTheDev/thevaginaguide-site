How to do Kegels correctly (most women get this wrong)

By Taja, women's health educator · November 12, 2026 · 6 min read

Quick answer

To do a Kegel, squeeze the muscles you'd use to stop gas and pee at the same time, lift them up and in, hold for 3 to 10 seconds while you keep breathing, then fully relax for the same amount of time. Getting the technique right matters: in one study only 49% of women did an ideal Kegel after verbal instructions, and 25% did something that could make leaking worse.

"Just do your Kegels." Thanks, but how? Most of us were told to squeeze, with zero explanation of what, where or how long. And that's a problem, because doing them wrong can actually work against you.

Why technique matters so much

In a classic study, women got brief verbal instructions and then had their Kegels measured. Only 49% did an ideal Kegel. Another 25% did a version that could promote leaking, usually by bearing down instead of lifting. So if Kegels haven't worked for you, your technique is the first thing to check.

Step 1: find your pelvic floor

  • The double stop: imagine stopping yourself from passing gas and stopping your pee at the same time.
  • The smoothie straw: picture your vagina sipping a smoothie through a straw. Squeeze, and draw up.
  • The finger check: put a clean finger just inside your vagina. When you squeeze, you should feel a gentle squeeze and lift around it.

Step 2: the perfect Kegel

  1. Lie on your back with knees bent. Relax your belly, butt and thighs.
  2. Breathe in and let everything soften.
  3. Breathe out, then squeeze and lift up and in. Keep breathing.
  4. Hold for 3 seconds to start, building up to 10.
  5. Fully let go for the same amount of time. The relax is half the exercise.

The 5 mistakes that sabotage results

  • Holding your breath. Always keep breathing.
  • Squeezing your butt, thighs or abs instead of the pelvic floor.
  • Bearing down or pushing out. That's the opposite of a Kegel.
  • Not relaxing fully between squeezes.
  • Doing them while you pee. Stopping your flow once to find the muscle is fine, but making it a habit can stop your bladder from emptying properly.

How many Kegels should you do?

Quality beats quantity. UK guidelines recommend a training program of at least 3 months as the first treatment for stress leaks. A simple, research-friendly starting point: about 8 to 10 slow holds plus 10 quick squeezes, 3 times a day. Muscles get tired too, so hundreds a day isn't better.

Does it actually work?

Yes. In a Cochrane review of 31 trials, women with stress leaks who did pelvic floor training were about 8 times more likely to report being cured than women who didn't (56% vs 6%). Most people notice a difference somewhere around 6 to 8 weeks of consistent practice.

Wait: should you be doing Kegels at all?

Not everyone needs more squeezing. If you have pain with sex, pain inserting a tampon, trouble starting to pee, or constipation, your pelvic floor may be too tight, and Kegels can make that worse. In that case, focus on relaxation and see a pelvic floor physical therapist. Can't feel anything after 2 to 3 weeks of trying? A PT can use biofeedback to show you exactly what your muscles are doing.

Questions people ask

How do I know if I'm doing Kegels right?

Put a clean finger just inside your vagina and squeeze. You should feel a gentle squeeze and an upward lift. If you feel pushing outward, or your butt and thighs are tightening, adjust your technique or see a pelvic floor physical therapist.

How long do Kegels take to work?

Most people notice changes around 6 to 8 weeks of consistent practice. Guidelines recommend at least 3 months of training for stress leaks.

Can Kegels make things worse?

Yes, if you bear down instead of lifting, or if your pelvic floor is already too tight. Signs of a tight pelvic floor include pain with sex, pain with tampons and trouble starting to pee.

How many Kegels a day should I do?

A common starting point is about 8 to 10 slow holds and 10 quick squeezes, 3 times a day. Good form matters more than high numbers.
