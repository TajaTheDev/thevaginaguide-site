Vaginal discharge color chart: what clear, white, yellow, green, gray and brown mean

By Taja Moxey, women's health educator · November 12, 2026 · 6 min read

Quick answer

Healthy discharge is usually clear, milky white or off-white, and it changes through your cycle. Thick, white and clumpy with itching can be yeast, thin gray with a fishy smell often points to BV, and yellow-green or frothy discharge can signal trichomoniasis or another STI. Read color, texture, smell and how you feel together, never color alone.

Let's be honest: you've looked at your underwear and Googled something like "is green discharge bad" at 1 a.m. No judgment. Discharge is one of the best health signals your body gives you, once you know how to read it.

First: discharge is a good thing

Discharge is how your vagina keeps itself clean and protected. It carries out old cells and helps guard against infection. The amount, color and texture change naturally with your cycle, arousal, exercise and hormones. Clear, milky white or off-white discharge is generally healthy. Changes in color, texture, smell or amount are what's worth paying attention to.

Why color alone can fool you

Cleveland Clinic notes that yellow, gray or green discharge may suggest a bacterial infection or an STI, but plenty of normal discharge dries yellowish on fabric. And infections overlap more than you'd think. In one study of women who were sure they had a yeast infection, only about a third actually had yeast alone. That's why the smart move is to read four things together:

  1. Color (what you see)
  2. Texture (watery, stretchy, creamy, clumpy, frothy)
  3. Smell (tangy is normal; fishy or foul is not)
  4. How you feel (itching, burning, pain, fever)

The color combos that mean "call a provider"

  • Gray, thin and fishy, especially after sex or your period (often BV)
  • Green, yellow-green or frothy (possible trich or another STI)
  • Thick and white with intense itching that doesn't clear with treatment
  • Bleeding between periods, after sex, or after menopause
  • Any unusual discharge while pregnant
  • Discharge plus pelvic pain, fever or chills

Questions people ask

What color discharge is normal?

Clear, milky white or off-white discharge is usually normal. Pale yellow without other symptoms is often normal too, since discharge can dry slightly yellow on underwear.

What does yellow discharge mean?

Pale yellow discharge with no smell or itching is often normal. Bright yellow, yellow-green or frothy discharge, especially with a bad smell or itching, can signal an infection like trichomoniasis and should be tested.

What does gray discharge mean?

Thin gray or off-white discharge with a fishy smell is a classic sign of bacterial vaginosis (BV), which needs prescription treatment.

Is brown discharge normal?

Brown discharge is usually old blood at the start or end of your period. See a provider if it happens between periods, after sex or after menopause.
