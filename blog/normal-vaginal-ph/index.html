What's a normal vaginal pH? How to test it at home (and when not to)

By Taja, women's health educator · November 12, 2026 · 5 min read

Quick answer

A normal vaginal pH is about 3.8 to 4.5, roughly as acidic as tomato juice. A reading above 4.5 can point to BV or trichomoniasis, while yeast infections usually leave pH normal. pH also rises naturally just before your period, after sex and after menopause, so test at the right time and treat the result as a clue, not a diagnosis.

"pH balanced" is printed on half the products in the feminine care aisle. But what is your vaginal pH supposed to be, and can you actually check it at home? Yes, and it's easier than you'd think.

What vaginal pH means

pH measures how acidic something is on a scale from 0 to 14. Lower means more acidic, 7 is neutral. A healthy vagina is typically between 3.8 and 4.5: acidic, thanks to lactic acid made by Lactobacillus. That acidity keeps unwanted bacteria from taking over.

What pushes pH up

  • Your period. Blood is less acidic, so pH rises during and just before your period.
  • Semen. It's alkaline, which is why pH can rise for a while after sex without a condom.
  • Douching and soaps inside the vagina. They wash away protective bacteria.
  • Menopause. Lower estrogen means fewer Lactobacillus, so pH above 4.5 can be normal after menopause.
  • BV and trichomoniasis. Both typically raise pH above 4.5.

How to test your vaginal pH at home

  1. Buy vaginal pH test strips or a swab kit made for vaginal use (not pool or aquarium strips).
  2. Wash your hands. Hold the strip against the side wall of your vagina for a few seconds, or use the swab as directed.
  3. Match the color to the chart right away.

When not to test: during your period, for a day or two after sex, or after using any vaginal medication, lube or douche. Those can all throw off the reading.

What your result may mean

ReadingWhat it may mean
3.8 to 4.5Normal for most people before menopause. If you're itchy with thick white discharge, yeast is possible, since yeast usually leaves pH normal.
Above 4.5Could be BV or trichomoniasis, especially with a fishy smell or thin gray discharge. Can also be normal around your period, after sex or after menopause.

What a home test can't tell you

A pH strip can't diagnose anything on its own. A normal reading doesn't rule out infections like yeast or some STIs, and a high reading doesn't tell BV and trich apart. Use it to decide whether to see a provider, and bring your result with you.

How to keep your pH happy

  • Skip douching and scented products. Water on the outside is enough.
  • Use condoms during and after BV treatment so semen doesn't keep raising your pH.
  • Change out of wet or sweaty clothes quickly.
  • Don't fall for "pH balancing" washes used inside the vagina. Your Lactobacillus handle that job.

Questions people ask

What is a normal vaginal pH?

About 3.8 to 4.5 for most people before menopause. It can be higher before menarche and after menopause.

What does a high vaginal pH mean?

A pH above 4.5 can point to bacterial vaginosis or trichomoniasis. It can also be temporary during your period or after sex.

Can a home pH test tell if I have a yeast infection?

Not on its own. Yeast infections usually leave pH normal, so a normal reading with itching and thick white discharge makes yeast more likely, but it isn't a diagnosis.

When should I not test my vaginal pH?

Avoid testing during your period, for a day or two after sex, and after using vaginal medication, lube or douches, since they can skew the result.
