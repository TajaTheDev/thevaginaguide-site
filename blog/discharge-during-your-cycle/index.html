Is my discharge normal? How discharge changes through your cycle

By Taja, women's health educator · November 12, 2026 · 5 min read

Quick answer

Yes, discharge changes on purpose. Right after your period it's often dry or sticky, then creamy, then clear, wet and stretchy like raw egg white around ovulation, then thicker and whiter again before your period. What isn't normal: a fishy or foul smell, gray, green or frothy discharge, cottage-cheese texture with itching, or burning and pain.

One week your underwear is basically dry. The next it looks like you sneezed egg whites. Then it's creamy, then it's gone again. If that's had you wondering whether something's wrong, here's the good news: that rollercoaster is your hormones doing exactly what they're supposed to.

Your cycle, week by week

Much of your discharge is cervical mucus, and estrogen and progesterone change it across the month. Here's a typical pattern for a 28-day cycle. Your days may be different, and that's okay.

Other things that change discharge (normally)

  • Arousal: clear, slippery and wet. That's natural lubrication.
  • Exercise: more clear or watery discharge.
  • Pregnancy: often more milky discharge than usual.
  • Hormonal birth control: can flatten the monthly pattern, since you may not ovulate.
  • Breastfeeding and menopause: lower estrogen often means less discharge and more dryness.

When discharge is not normal

Normal discharge has a mild smell and changes in predictable ways. Cleveland Clinic advises contacting a provider when you notice changes to what's normal for you, like a new color, texture, smell or amount. Specifically, watch for:

  • A fishy smell, especially after sex or your period (often BV)
  • Gray, green, yellow-green or frothy discharge
  • Thick, cottage-cheese discharge with itching or burning (possible yeast)
  • Bleeding between periods or after sex
  • Pain, fever or burning when you pee

The trick: learn your normal

The best way to spot a problem early is to know your own pattern. For one or two cycles, jot down your cycle day, what your discharge looks like, and any smell. Questions people ask

Is it normal to have discharge every day?

Yes. Most people have some discharge most days, and the amount changes through the cycle. More discharge around ovulation and before your period is common.

What does egg white discharge mean?

Clear, slippery, stretchy discharge like raw egg white usually means you're close to ovulation, your most fertile time.

Why do I have so much discharge before my period?

Hormone shifts before your period can increase white, creamy discharge. If it smells fishy or foul, or comes with itching, get it checked.

When should I worry about discharge?

See a provider for a fishy or foul smell, gray, green or frothy discharge, cottage-cheese discharge with itching, bleeding between periods, or pain, fever or burning.
