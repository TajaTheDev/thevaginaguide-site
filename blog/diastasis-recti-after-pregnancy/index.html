Ab gap after pregnancy? How to check for diastasis recti and what helps

By Taja Moxey, women's health educator · November 20, 2026 · 6 min read

Quick answer

Diastasis recti is a gap between the two sides of your abs that opens up to make room for your baby. It's extremely common: in one study, 60% of first-time moms had one at 6 weeks after birth, and about 1 in 3 still did at 12 months. You can check yours at home by lifting your head while lying down and feeling for a gap at your belly button. Gentle breathing, deep core work and avoiding doming help it heal. A firm, controlled middle matters more than a perfectly closed gap.

Your baby is here, but your belly still pokes out, especially when you sit up. It might even form a ridge down the middle. Before you blame yourself or your diet: there's a good chance your abs have separated. It's called diastasis recti, and it's one of the most common things that happens to your body in pregnancy.

What is diastasis recti?

Your "six-pack" muscles run down the front of your belly in two halves, joined by a strip of connective tissue called the linea alba. As your baby grows, that tissue stretches and the two sides move apart to make room. After birth, the gap often stays for a while.

60%had an ab gap at 6 weeks postpartum
45%still had one at 6 months
1 in 3still had one at 12 months

Those numbers come from a study that followed 300 first-time moms in Norway. The takeaway: it's normal, and for many women it narrows a lot in the first year.

How to check your ab gap at home

  1. Lie on your back with your knees bent and feet flat.
  2. Put your fingertips sideways across your belly button.
  3. Lift your head and shoulders slightly off the floor.
  4. Feel for a gap between the two sides of your abs. Count how many fingers fit, and notice how deep they sink.
  5. Check a few inches above and below your belly button too.

What matters most isn't only the width. Notice whether the middle feels firm or soft, and whether your belly domes (pokes up into a ridge) when you sit up. A soft, deep gap or doming is a sign to go slower and get checked.

What helps it heal

  • Breathe first. Breathe in and let your belly and pelvic floor relax. Breathe out and gently lift your pelvic floor and draw your lower belly in. This "connection breath" is the base of every core exercise after birth.
  • Exhale on effort. Lifting your baby, the car seat or a laundry basket? Breathe out and lift your pelvic floor first. Never hold your breath and strain.
  • Start with deep core moves. Heel slides, dead bugs with one leg at a time, bridges and bird dogs train the deep muscles without bulging the middle.
  • Watch for doming. If your belly ridges during any exercise, make it easier: fewer reps, smaller movement, or go back a step.
  • Roll to get up. Getting out of bed straight up like a sit-up puts pressure on the gap. Roll to your side and push up with your arms.
  • Hold off on crunches, sit-ups and full planks until the middle feels firm when you lift your head.

Leaking, heaviness or pain alongside your ab gap? Your pelvic floor is part of the same team. Our guide to doing Kegels correctly is a good place to start.

Is it ever too late?

No. Whether you're 6 weeks or 6 years postpartum, your deep core still responds to training. The goal isn't a perfectly closed gap. It's a strong, controlled middle that supports you when you lift, run and play.

See a provider or pelvic floor physio if

  • The gap is wider than 2 to 3 fingers, or feels soft and deep, at 3 months postpartum
  • You have a bulge that stays when you relax, or pain at your belly button (possible hernia)
  • You have back pain, leaking, or heaviness in your vagina
  • You had a C-section and your scar is still painful or pulling after several months

Questions people ask

How do I know if I have diastasis recti?

Lie on your back with knees bent, lift your head slightly and feel for a gap between your abs at your belly button. A gap you can fit fingers into, a soft middle, or a ridge (doming) when you sit up are signs of diastasis recti.

Does diastasis recti go away on its own?

Often it narrows a lot. In one study, 60% of first-time moms had a gap at 6 weeks postpartum, falling to about 1 in 3 at 12 months. Targeted core training helps.

Can I do crunches with diastasis recti?

Hold off on crunches, sit-ups and full planks until the middle feels firm and doesn't dome. Start with breathing, heel slides, dead bugs and bridges.

When should I see someone about my ab gap?

If the gap is wider than 2 to 3 fingers or soft at 3 months, you have a bulge or pain at your belly button, or you also have leaking or heaviness, see your provider or a pelvic floor physical therapist.
