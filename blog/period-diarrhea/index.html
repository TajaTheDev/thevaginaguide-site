Period poops are real: why your period gives you diarrhea (and how to calm it)

By Taja, women's health educator · November 12, 2026 · 5 min read

Quick answer

Prostaglandins, the chemicals that make your uterus contract during your period, can make your bowels contract too, which is why many people get loose stools on their period. In one study of healthy women, 73% had at least one gut symptom before their period and about 1 in 4 had diarrhea. Starting an NSAID a day or two before your period and eating steady soluble fiber can help.

Nobody warned us that our periods would come with a side of urgent bathroom trips. If your bathroom blows up every month, you are extremely not alone, and there's a real, science-y reason for it.

Meet the culprit: prostaglandins

Right before and during your period, the lining of your uterus releases prostaglandins. Their job is to make your uterus contract so it can shed its lining. Cramps? That's them. But prostaglandins don't stay politely in the uterus. They can also make the smooth muscle of your bowels contract more, which speeds things up and leads to loose stools. Doctors note the same chemicals behind cramps can bring nausea and diarrhea along for the ride.

How common are period poops?

Very. In a study of 156 healthy women with no gut conditions, 73% had at least one gut symptom like belly pain, diarrhea or constipation before their period. Diarrhea hit 24% before their period and 28% during it.

What helps

  1. Get ahead of prostaglandins. NSAIDs like ibuprofen or naproxen lower prostaglandin production. If they're safe for you, guidance suggests starting 1 to 2 days before your period and continuing for the first 2 to 3 days. Many people find this calms cramps and bowels at the same time.
  2. Steady soluble fiber all month. Soluble fiber (oats, chia, bananas, psyllium, beans) absorbs water and firms things up. The trick is consistency. Suddenly eating a mountain of fiber on day 1 can make bloating worse. This is the smart version of fibermaxxing: build up slowly and drink water.
  3. Hydrate. Diarrhea pulls water out of your body. Water and an electrolyte drink help.
  4. Go easy on triggers. Coffee, alcohol, spicy and very greasy foods can all speed your gut up. Notice what makes your period week worse.
  5. Heat. A heating pad on your belly can ease both cramps and gut cramping.

When period poops aren't normal

See a provider if you have severe pain with bowel movements during your period, blood in your stool, diarrhea that lasts all month, weight loss, or pain that's getting worse each cycle. These can be signs of endometriosis, IBS or other conditions that are very treatable once diagnosed.

Questions people ask

Why do I poop more on my period?

Prostaglandins released by the uterus to trigger contractions can also make your bowels contract, which speeds digestion and can cause loose stools.

How do I stop period diarrhea?

If NSAIDs are safe for you, starting one 1 to 2 days before your period lowers prostaglandins. Eating steady soluble fiber through the month, staying hydrated and limiting coffee and alcohol also help.

Is diarrhea on your period normal?

Yes, it's common. In one study about 1 in 4 healthy women had diarrhea before or during their period. Severe pain, blood in stool or symptoms all month are reasons to see a provider.

Does fiber help period poops?

Soluble fiber can firm up loose stools, but add it gradually through the month. A sudden big increase on day 1 can make bloating worse.
