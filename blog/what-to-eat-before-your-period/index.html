What to eat the week before your period (to cramp less and bloat less)

By Taja, women's health educator · November 12, 2026 · 6 min read

Quick answer

In the week before your period, lean on calcium-rich foods, ginger, fiber and water, and ease up on salt. In a large trial, 1,200 mg of calcium a day cut PMS symptoms by 48% compared with 30% on placebo, and a meta-analysis found ginger reduced period pain. Starting an NSAID a day or two before your period can also prevent cramps if it's safe for you.

The week before your period can feel like your body is staging a hostile takeover: cramps, bloating, cravings, crying at commercials. Food won't fix everything, but a few choices have real research behind them, and they're easy wins.

Why the week before matters

As your period approaches, hormones drop and your uterus lining starts making prostaglandins, the chemicals that make it contract. More prostaglandins means stronger cramps (and sometimes period poops). PMS symptoms like bloating and mood swings also peak in this window. So the week before is when prevention pays off.

Calcium: the PMS MVP

In a randomized trial of nearly 500 women with moderate to severe PMS, taking 1,200 mg of calcium a day for three cycles reduced total symptoms by 48%, compared with 30% on placebo. That included mood swings, bloating, cravings and aches. Food first: a cup of yogurt has roughly 300 to 450 mg. If you're thinking about a supplement, check with your provider, especially if you take iron (calcium blocks iron absorption when taken together).

Ginger: the cramp helper

A meta-analysis of randomized trials found that 750 to 2,000 mg of ginger powder in the first 3 to 4 days of the cycle reduced period pain compared with placebo. Ginger tea counts as a cozy start, though studies used capsules with measured doses.

The real cramp game-changer: timing your pain reliever

If NSAIDs like ibuprofen or naproxen are safe for you, timing matters. Family medicine guidance recommends starting them 1 to 2 days before your period and continuing through the first 2 to 3 days, because they lower prostaglandin production. Waiting until the pain peaks means you're playing catch-up.

What about chocolate?

Honestly? Have the chocolate. Dark chocolate has some magnesium and a lot of emotional support. Just pair it with the stuff above.

When food isn't enough

See a provider if cramps make you miss work or school, pain is getting worse over time, bleeding is very heavy, or you have pain with sex or bowel movements. Conditions like endometriosis and fibroids are common and treatable, and severe pain is not something you have to just push through.

Questions people ask

What should I eat to reduce period cramps?

Ginger has the best research for cramps, and fiber, water and less salt help with bloating. Calcium-rich foods help with overall PMS symptoms. Starting an NSAID a day or two before your period, if it's safe for you, prevents cramps best.

Does calcium help PMS?

Yes. In a large randomized trial, 1,200 mg of calcium a day reduced PMS symptoms by 48% compared with 30% on placebo after three cycles.

What foods make period bloating worse?

Salty processed foods make your body hold onto water. Some people also notice more bloating from carbonated drinks and alcohol.

When should I start eating for my period?

About 7 days before your period is expected, when PMS symptoms typically start building.
