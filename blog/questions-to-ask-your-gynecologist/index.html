11 questions to ask your gynecologist about vaginal symptoms

By Taja, women's health educator · November 12, 2026 · 6 min read

Quick answer

Before your appointment, write down your symptoms, when they started, your last period and every product you use, and skip douching, vaginal creams, tampons and sex for a day or two so tests are accurate. In the room, ask what's causing it, whether they can test to confirm, how long treatment lasts, whether your partner needs treatment, and what the plan is if it comes back.

You waited three weeks for the appointment. You get seven minutes. The doctor says "probably yeast," hands you a prescription, and you're back in the car before you remember the three things you meant to ask. Let's fix that.

Before your visit: set yourself up

  • Book smart. If a swab is likely, try to schedule when you're not on your period, unless bleeding is the issue.
  • Hands off for 24 to 48 hours. Skip douching, vaginal creams or suppositories, tampons and sex. They can change test results.
  • Write it down. When symptoms started, your last period, every product you use down there, and every medication, including antibiotics in the last 3 months.
  • Photograph rashes or sores. They may look different by the time you're seen.
  • Ask for what you need. You can request a female provider or a chaperone.

The 11 questions

  1. What do you think is causing this, and how sure are you?
  2. Can we test to confirm it instead of guessing? (swab, pH, culture, STI tests)
  3. Could this be more than one thing at once?
  4. Should I have an STI test today?
  5. What's the treatment, how long do I take it, and what are the side effects?
  6. Is it safe with my birth control, other medications, or if I could be pregnant?
  7. Does my partner need to be tested or treated too?
  8. What should I avoid while I'm treating it? (sex, alcohol, certain products)
  9. When should I feel better, and when should I call if I don't?
  10. Do I need a follow-up test after treatment?
  11. If it comes back, what's the plan? Is there a longer or preventive option?

Why "can we test it?" is the most important question

Vaginal symptoms overlap a lot. In a study of women who had diagnosed themselves with a yeast infection, only about a third had yeast alone; others had BV, mixed infections, trich, or nothing at all. The CDC's guidance for BV, for example, describes diagnosis using an exam plus lab tests on a sample of vaginal fluid. Asking for testing isn't being difficult. It's how you get the right treatment the first time.

Why the partner question matters

For BV in particular, the science changed recently. A 2025 trial found that treating male partners at the same time cut BV recurrence from 63% to 35%. If you have a regular partner and BV keeps coming back, it's a fair question. Details in should your partner be treated for BV?

Before you leave the parking lot

Write down the exact diagnosis, what tests were done and when results come back, the treatment name, dose and number of days, what to avoid, and your follow-up date. Future you, three months from now, will thank you.

Questions people ask

What should I not do before a gynecologist appointment?

For 24 to 48 hours before, skip douching, vaginal creams or suppositories, tampons and sex, since they can change test results.

Can I ask my doctor to test for a yeast infection?

Yes. Asking for a swab or culture to confirm the cause is reasonable, especially if symptoms keep coming back or treatment hasn't worked.

Should I go to the gynecologist on my period?

If a swab is likely, try to book when you're not on your period, unless bleeding is the reason for the visit.

What should I bring to a gynecologist appointment?

A list of symptoms and when they started, your last period date, every product you use down there, your medications, and your questions.
